Les Viaducs de la Seine-et Oise

2026-10-29

I don’t exactly know why I decided to start my Marguerite Duras journey there, but I picked Les Viaducs de la Seine-et Oise as my first reading in her collected works. It is not exactly my cup of tea, as I struggle with enjoying reading the texts of plays in general, whether Shakeseare’s Titus Andronicus or even my beloved Tennessee Williams that I would rather watch than read. This particular play has layers of repetition mixed with echolaliac content that may be either misunderstood by me, or be simply pretentious.

However, I was intrigued to learn that it is inspired by a true story of a body dismembered and thrown into various railcars to be distributed to remote locations, exactly the same plot device as used in the film Black Coal, Thin Ice by Diao Yinan, leading me to wonder if Diao Yinan had the same inspiration. I am going to resist going into a deep dive on this one to find out, but will only note that the film, with the enigmatic Gwei Lun-Mei highlighted in a number of striking scenes, finds an elegiac tone that transcends the literal notes of the crime plot. It is a true piece of cinema with moods expressed in iconic imagery all the more impressive for occurring in everyday locations like a laundry.

That film is both a model and a point of jealousy for me. If I were able to achieve in writing just a handful of those perfectly poised moments, it would be enough to justify the effort.

Going back to the play, it contains this interesting line.

Il ne faut pas demander trop au monde. On ne peut pas à la fois avoir compris, ne plus comprendre et comprendre encore ce qu’on ne comprend plus.

Which is to me also somewhat circular and mystifying, yet leaves one wondering whether one could manage to express it any better, this sense of not finding anything at the end of a long search, and yet believing that the not finding of anything is its own answer to the seeking.
